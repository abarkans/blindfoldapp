You've got a call booked for 9pm. You've been looking forward to it all day. Then it starts, and within ten minutes it's turned into a status meeting: work was fine, the flat's cold, did you eat. Forty minutes later you hang up feeling oddly further apart than before you dialled.

That's the quiet problem with long distance. It isn't the distance itself. It's that every evening together runs through the same format: a screen and "so, how was your day?" Long distance date night games fix the format. They give the call something to do besides report back, and they put a bit of surprise into a relationship that mostly runs on schedules.

---

## The call isn't the problem. The format is.

Here's the encouraging part. In a week-long diary study of 63 dating couples, Jiang and Hancock found that [long-distance couples reported more intimacy in their interactions](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcom.12029) than couples who lived close by. The driver wasn't grand gestures. Long-distance partners disclosed more about themselves, and they read each other's behaviour more generously. Distance, handled well, pushed them to say more.

That's the lever the games below pull. A status update tells your partner what happened. A good game makes you reveal something: how you think, what you'd pick for them, what you'd draw if nobody gave you the full brief. It's a small study, so don't read it as proof that distance is secretly better. Read it as proof that a call can carry real closeness, if you give it a job.

## Long distance date night games that cost nothing

### 1. The cook-along

Pick one recipe. Both of you shop for it, prop the phone against the kettle, and cook at the same time. You'll be at different stages within five minutes and narrating each other's mistakes within ten. Then eat together on camera, at a table, not on the bed.

### 2. The secret delivery

Each of you orders a small surprise to the other's door without saying what it is: a dessert, a coffee, the snack they mentioned once three weeks ago. Time it to land during the call. You get the reveal live on camera, which is the closest thing to being in the room.

### 3. The voice-only round

Cameras off. Ask each other three questions (a favourite memory, a worst habit, something you've never admitted) and guess how the other person reacted from tone alone. It's lifted from our [blindfold date guide](/blog/blindfold-date-ideas-ultimate-guide), and it works even better over a phone line. A phone line is already a blindfold.

### 4. The parallel walk

Go outside at the same time, earbuds in, call running. You each get fifteen minutes to find one thing on your street the other would love, and describe it without showing it. Then flip the camera and see how close the description was. You'll end up knowing each other's neighbourhoods better than you'd expect.

### 5. The rabbit-hole swap

Each of you gets five minutes of screen-share to show the other the most interesting thing you found this week: a documentary clip, a strange Wikipedia page, a song, a map. No explaining in advance. The only rule is that it has to be something you genuinely wanted them to see.

### 6. The prediction pact

Start the same film at the same moment. Before you press play, each of you writes three predictions on paper: who's lying, who ends up together, how it ends. No sharing. At the credits, hold your papers up to the camera. Whoever got the fewest right picks next week's film.

## Three rules that make a call feel like a date

**Book it like a date.** Not "call you later." A set time and a named game, agreed earlier in the day. Deciding what to do once you're both already on camera is how calls slide back into status updates.

**Change one thing about the setting.** A different room, a real glass, a shirt that isn't the one you slept in. It sounds trivial. It's the signal that this call is different from the other six this week, the same trick that makes [date night ideas at home](/blog/date-night-ideas-at-home) feel like an occasion instead of a default.

**No second screen.** The moment one of you is half-reading email, the other can tell. Laptop closed, notifications off, for the length of one game at least.

## Frequently asked questions

### What can long distance couples do on a video call?

Anything with a shared task beats open-ended chat. Cook the same recipe at the same time, start the same film and make predictions, take a parallel walk, or play a game where each of you has a brief the other hasn't seen. The task gives the call a shape, so it doesn't drift into logistics.

### How do you make a long distance date feel special?

Treat it like a date rather than a check-in. Book a time in advance, change something about your setting, close every other screen, and add one element of surprise, such as a delivery the other doesn't know about or a sealed mission you open together.

### Do printable date games work if we live in different countries?

Yes. The packs are PDFs with a separate copy for each partner, so you simply send your partner their half and each of you prints or opens your own wherever you are. No account is needed to buy or download.

### Are long distance date night games only for new couples?

No. Couples who've been apart for months often need them more, because the calls have settled into a routine. A game is one of the easiest ways to put something new into a relationship that runs mostly through a screen.
